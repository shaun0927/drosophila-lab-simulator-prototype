# 0. Competition Scope

## Product frame

**Title:** Drosophila Lab Simulator: Publish or Perish

**One-sentence pitch:** Engineer absurd Drosophila neural traits, discover a bizarre behavior, package it as a suspiciously publishable figure, survive Reviewer #2, and submit before midnight.

## Player identity

The player is a deadline-haunted Drosophila researcher. They are not merely performing correct lab procedure. They are caught between discovery, career pressure, and the temptation to over-sell a weird result.

## Target session

- First play: 5 minutes
- Repeat play: 3 minutes
- Demo video: one complete loop visible within 3 minutes

## Competition goal

Discover one publishable abnormal fly behavior before midnight and submit a manuscript with the strongest possible ending.

## Core fantasy

> “This result is absurd. Can I make it look rigorous enough to publish?”

## Core tension

> Weird enough to be exciting, credible enough to survive review.

The player’s main decision is whether to make the finding more believable or more sensational.

## Core loop

```text
Trait Cards
→ Mutant Line
→ Behavior Discovery
→ Assay Trials
→ Figure Framing
→ Reviewer Attack
→ Submission Result
```

## Core stats

| Stat | Meaning | Player feeling |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence | How much experimental support exists | “We have data.” |
| Weirdness | How strange the behavior is | “This could be big.” |
| Credibility | How believable the claim appears | “Reviewers might accept this.” |
| Hype | How attention-grabbing the story is | “People will click this.” |
| Suspicion | Risk of reviewer, ethics, or replication disaster | “This might blow up.” |
| Time | Deadline pressure | “Can I afford one more assay?” |
| Budget | Resource pressure | “Can I afford the safer option?” |

## Core dilemmas

1. **Weirdness vs credibility**
   - bizarre findings are exciting but harder to believe.
2. **More assay vs submit now**
   - more evidence costs time and can trigger new problems.
3. **Conservative figure vs big claim**
   - careful framing survives review; aggressive framing creates hype and suspicion.

## Competition content limits

- Trait cards: 9
- Discoverable phenomena: 6 named + generic fallback
- Assay actions: 3
- Figure framing options: 4
- Reviewer attacks: 5
- Submission endings: 6
- One playable run: ≤ 5 minutes

## Trait cards

### Neural

1. **Blue Light Switch**
   - Tags: `light`, `neural`
   - Increases Weirdness.
   - Enables light-triggered phenomena.

2. **Mushroom Body Boost**
   - Tags: `memory`, `neural`
   - Increases Credibility and Weirdness.
   - Enables learning/memory phenomena.

3. **Anxiety Loop**
   - Tags: `anxiety`, `loop`
   - Increases Weirdness and Suspicion.
   - Enables repetitive/avoidance phenomena.

### Motor

4. **Hyperactive Motor Circuit**
   - Tags: `hyperactive`, `motor`
   - Makes behavior easier to observe but noisier.

5. **Sleep Deprivation**
   - Tags: `sleep`, `stress`
   - Increases Weirdness but lowers Credibility.

6. **Wall Climber**
   - Tags: `wall`, `motor`
   - Enables spatial/locomotor visuals.

### Social/reward

7. **Sugar Memory**
   - Tags: `sugar`, `memory`, `reward`
   - Increases Credibility.

8. **Courtship Confusion**
   - Tags: `courtship`, `social`
   - High Weirdness, higher Suspicion.

9. **Social Bias**
   - Tags: `social`, `swarm`
   - Increases Hype.
   - Enables group phenomena.

## Discoverable phenomena

| Required tags | Phenomenon | Base identity |
|---|---|---|
| `light` + `hyperactive` + `social/swarm` | Light-Induced Swarm Dance | Best first-run visual |
| `sugar/reward` + `anxiety` + `sleep/stress` | Compulsive Foraging Spiral | Repetitive behavior |
| `courtship` + `memory/reward` | Romantic Misdirection | Comic social behavior |
| `memory` + `social/swarm` | Collective False Memory | Neuroscience flavor |
| `sleep/stress` + `wall` | Midnight Wall Parade | Spatial visual |
| `light` + `courtship` | Phototactic Courtship Disaster | Comic light/social failure |

Fallback: **Ambiguous Locomotor Weirdness** for unsupported combinations.

## Figure framing options

1. **Conservative Figure**
   - Title style: “Blue Light Modulates Locomotor Synchrony”
   - Credibility up, Hype down, Suspicion down.

2. **Big Claim Figure**
   - Title style: “A Neural Switch for Collective Decision-Making”
   - Hype up, Weirdness up, Suspicion up.

3. **Beautiful But Vague Figure**
   - Title style: “Emergent Behavioral Dynamics in Engineered Flies”
   - Hype up, mild Credibility up, Suspicion up.

4. **Data Massage**
   - Title style: “Robust Evidence for Social Phototaxis”
   - Evidence/Credibility up, Suspicion sharply up.
   - Treated as satire/risk, not endorsement.

## Reviewer attacks

1. **Sample Size Attack** — “n=12 is not a sample size. It is a rumor.”
2. **Control Attack** — “Where is the heat-control experiment?”
3. **Mechanism Attack** — “Correlation is not neural circuitry.”
4. **Replication Attack** — “Can anyone reproduce this?”
5. **Ethics Attack** — “Why are the flies forming committees?”

## Endings

1. **Breakthrough** — high Evidence, Weirdness, Credibility; low Suspicion.
2. **Viral Preprint** — high Hype, tolerable Suspicion.
3. **Solid but Boring** — high Evidence/Credibility, low Weirdness.
4. **Desk Rejected** — low Evidence or Credibility.
5. **Replication Crisis** — high Hype/Suspicion, weak Evidence.
6. **Ethics Committee Summoned** — extreme Weirdness/Suspicion.
